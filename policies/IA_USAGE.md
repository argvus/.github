# ARGVUS / argvus* AI Usage Policy

## Community and Scope

The ARGVUS project recognizes that AI tools, including large language models
(LLMs), can be useful for software development. This policy defines when
AI-assisted material may be included in contributions to the ARGVUS ecosystem.

This policy governs contributions submitted to ARGVUS repositories. It does
not generally prohibit contributors from using AI for their own work, learning,
research, or development outside the project.

## Acceptable Contributions

ARGVUS will decline contributions that are legally significant for copyright
purposes and that include content generated by an LLM or are derived from
LLM-generated content.

Maintainers may accept legally insignificant contributions generated by an
LLM, provided that they satisfy the project's ordinary contribution
requirements and are clearly identified as AI-assisted.

As an explicit exception, maintainers may accept legally significant
contributions to test cases that were generated in whole or in part by an LLM.

The fact that a contribution is useful, correct, tested, or fully understood by
the submitter does not by itself make AI-generated production code eligible
under this policy.

## Understanding the 15-Line Guideline

The GNU maintainer guidance describes a change of fewer than roughly 15 lines
as generally not legally significant. This is a rule of thumb, not a universal
legal threshold and not an automatic permission to submit any AI-generated
snippet of that size.

When assessing whether a contribution is legally insignificant, maintainers
may consider the nature and originality of the material, the change as a whole,
and the contributor's related contributions over time.

Examples and qualifications:

- A small change of a few lines will often be legally insignificant.
- A mechanical change, such as consistently renaming a symbol, may remain
  insignificant even if it affects many lines.
- Several individually small contributions by the same contributor may add up
  to a legally significant contribution.
- Ideas, bug reports, and suggestions without contributed code or text are not
  themselves copyright-significant code contributions.
- A short but original or expressive passage may still require closer review.

Contributors must not split one contribution into multiple patches, pull
requests, commits, or files to evade this policy. Maintainers may assess related
changes together.

## Tests

AI-generated tests may be accepted without a line limit, including when the
test contribution is legally significant, at the maintainers' discretion.

Tests must follow the project's conventions, exercise the intended behavior,
and be submitted for human review. Tests must not be used to disguise or
smuggle AI-generated production code into a contribution.

Acceptance of AI-generated tests does not make associated AI-generated
production code eligible for acceptance.

## Personal Use of AI

This policy does not generally restrict a contributor's personal use of AI for
purposes such as:

- Learning, explanation, and summarization.
- Research, technical analysis, and exploring possible approaches.
- Bug discovery and debugging.
- Reviewing a patch as an aid to human review.
- Accessibility assistance, direct translation, spelling, and grammar
  assistance, where the contributor verifies the result.

These uses remain outside the contribution restriction only when their
generated output is not incorporated into the submitted contribution. If
AI-generated output is copied, adapted, or otherwise included in the
contribution, the contribution policy applies to that material.

Bug reports and descriptions of findings may be submitted by a human after
appropriate consideration. AI output must not be sent verbatim without due
consideration.

## Transparency and Accountability

Every contribution containing AI-generated content that is submitted for
acceptance must clearly disclose that use.

The commit message must include an `Assisted-by:` tag identifying the AI tool
or model used. The accompanying description must explain, in practical terms,
which parts of the contribution were generated or derived from AI output.

A vague disclosure such as "used AI" is insufficient when it does not make
the AI's role clear. Contributors must not conceal AI use or misrepresent
generated material as entirely human-written.

Every contribution must be submitted by a human who understands the changes
and is prepared to answer questions about them. The decision to include a
contribution in ARGVUS must also be made by a human. An AI system may not
submit or commit a contribution on its own behalf.

Maintainers may ask for clarification about the origin of a change and may
decline a contribution when its provenance or eligibility cannot be
sufficiently established.

## Contributor Responsibility

The contributor is responsible for the submitted work, including its
correctness, licensing compatibility, provenance, and compliance with this
policy.

Reviewing generated code, observing that tests pass, or asking an LLM to
explain the code does not by itself establish that the contributor understands
the change.

Before submitting, contributors must be able to explain the purpose, logic,
structure, and important trade-offs of their changes without relying on an
LLM to supply those explanations.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will assess possible violations in context and may ask the
contributor for clarification or additional information.

Depending on the circumstances, responses may include a request to revise or
withdraw a contribution, rejection of the contribution, a warning, or
restrictions on future participation. Deliberate concealment, repeated
circumvention, or repeated submission of ineligible material may result in a
ban from ARGVUS project spaces.

The project will apply this policy consistently and may update it as its
practices and the relevant legal or technical landscape evolve.
